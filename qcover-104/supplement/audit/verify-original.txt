**Independent verification, 11 November 2026 (UTC).**

**Witness verdict: confirmed.** The supplied certificate proves
\(C_2(6,3,2)\le104\). My independently written checker verifies 104 distinct
three-dimensional subspaces and coverage of all 651 two-dimensional subspaces.
It imports none of the searcher's code or incidence data.

**Priority verdict: provisionally new.** I found no previously published or
arXiv-posted bound of 104 or better for these parameters, including the dual
and complementary packing formulations. The smallest upper bound I located
and checked in primary mathematical text is 105. I found no published lower
bound stronger than 99. Thus **99–104 is the interval supported by this audit**;
104 is not proved optimal. This is a substantial bounded literature check,
not a guarantee that no unindexed predecessor exists. In particular, the live
Bayreuth site could not be reached.

**What was searched.** I searched the exact parameter strings with several
spacing conventions, q-covering designs, q-Turán designs, planes covering

the historical tables, later papers, named authors' publication lists, and
arXiv material through the audit date. Live OpenAlex responses returned all
13 indexed citers of Etzion 2014, both citers of Cossidente–Pavese 2016, and the

**The duality matters.** For the ordinary dot product on \(V=\mathbb F_2^6\),
\(U\subseteq B\) if and only if \(B^\perp\subseteq U^\perp\). Therefore the
equivalent problem selects 3-spaces so that **each 4-space contains a selected
3-space**. In the usual convention this is
\[
C_2(6,3,2)=T_2(6,4,3).
\]
Etzion–Vardy state the general equivalence in Theorem 2.1 of
[*On q-analogs of Steiner systems and covering designs*](https://etzion.net.technion.ac.il/files/2016/02/P82.pdf).
In projective terminology it is a **(3,2)-blocking set in PG(5,2)**: selected
planes contained in every solid. Pavese 2020 orders the last two Turán
parameters the other way, writing this as \(T_2(6,3,4)\). It is **not** the
covering number \(C_2(6,4,3)\), which asks for solids covering planes.

There is another useful search translation. There are 1,395 ambient 3-spaces,
and each 2-space is in 15 of them. Complementing a cover gives a packing in
which each 2-space is in at most 14 blocks. Using the notation of
[Etzion–Kurz–Otal–Özbudak](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06081) and
[Qian–Wang–Xie–Ge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09277),
\[
C_2(6,3,2)=1395-A_2(6,3,2;14)=1395-B_2(6,3,2;15).
\]
Thus a predecessor to this result could also be a packing/code of size at
least **1,291**. I checked this terminology as well.

**The lower bound.** Independently of the literature, fix a nonzero point
\(P\). The 31 two-spaces through \(P\) correspond to the points of
\(V/P\cong\mathbb F_2^5\). Each selected 3-space through \(P\) covers just
three of them, so at least \(\lceil31/3\rceil=11\) selected blocks contain
\(P\). Counting incidences with all 63 points gives
\[
7|\mathcal B|\ge63\cdot11=693,
\qquad |\mathcal B|\ge99.
\]
This is the q-Schönheim lower bound, consistent with the table above and
[Etzion–Vardy, Theorem 5.1](https://etzion.net.technion.ac.il/files/2016/02/P82.pdf).
The present audit found no published exclusion of sizes 99–103 and proves
none.

**Automorphisms: an exact additional finding.** The full stabilizer of this
block family in \(\mathrm{GL}(6,2)=\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(6,2)\) has order **4**
and is **\(C_2\times C_2\)**. Two generators are specified below by their
images of the standard basis vectors encoded as \(1,2,4,8,16,32\), in that
order:

```text
g: [15, 29, 10, 25, 30, 49]
h: [39, 36, 51, 25, 54, 49]
```

Both are involutions, they commute, and their four products are precisely
the stabilizer. Its point orbits have sizes \(1^7,2^{12},4^8\); its block
orbits have sizes \(1^8,2^{24},4^{12}\).

This is an exhaustive determination, not just a discovered subgroup.
I coloured points by block degree, coloured each pair by the cover
multiplicity of its joining line, and repeatedly refined point colours by
the counts of coloured neighbours. Every stabilizing linear map preserves
these colours. For the domain basis \((17,14,38,5,1,2)\), this reduces all
possible image bases to 4,096 candidates. I explicitly tested every candidate
against the complete block family; exactly four passed. The code also checks
that the reported generators generate all four maps. See
[automorphisms.py](automorphisms.py) and [automorphisms.json](automorphisms.json).

The seven points with block degree greater than 11 are
\(\{14,17,31,38,40,55,57\}\). They form the plane
\(E=\langle14,17,38\rangle\), which the stabilizer fixes pointwise and which
is not itself selected. The 104 blocks meet \(E\) in respectively zero, one
and three nonzero points with frequencies **48, 28, 28**. The remaining 56
points all have degree 11; the degrees on \(E\) are \(12^2,16^3,20^2\).
There is no additional correlation stabilizing the family: 36 four-spaces
contain no original block, whereas the orthogonal-complement family has the
Turán property. A correlation preserving this cover would force that property
on the original family too.
